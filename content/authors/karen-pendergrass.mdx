Karen Pendergrass is the founder and editor-in-chief of Heavy Metal Facts, a translational microbiome scientist, and a standards architect working at the intersection of the gut microbiome, trace metals, and chronic disease. She is the co-founder of the Paleo Foundation, Microbiome Medicine, Microbiome Signatures, and the Heavy Metal Tested & Certified program. Her research focuses on developing microbiome-metallomic signatures and translating complex contaminant science into evidence-based reporting, certification frameworks, and regulatory tools.

## Background

Karen studied Business Administration at the University of Missouri-Kansas City's Henry W. Bloch School of Management (2006–2010) and completed coursework in gut microbiome science through the University of Colorado Boulder. Between 1997 and 2003, she served as an assistant circulator at the University of Kansas Hospital and as a circulating nurse and translator for Medical Aid for Children of Latin America (MACLA), providing pro bono pediatric reconstructive plastic surgery support. From 2005 to 2009, she served as a guest student evaluator in the Department of Molecular Biosciences at the University of Kansas.

In 2012, based on her own investigative research, Karen became the first documented case of fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT) for celiac disease—four years before the first published case study—an experience that catalyzed her trajectory into microbiome research and translational medicine.

## Microbiome Research & Microbial Metallomics

Karen is the founder of the Microbiome Signatures Database, a clinician- and researcher-facing platform that formalizes disease-associated microbiome patterns through Major Microbial Associations (MMAs). The database facilitates intervention development using a structured Microbial Shift and Realignment Process (MSRP) and a dual-alignment validation model.

She has led foundational work in microbial metallomics, a field she pioneered to integrate trace metal analysis into microbiome research. Her metallomic research examines how microorganisms differentially acquire, utilize, and detoxify trace elements—including nickel, zinc, iron, cadmium, lead, and aluminum—and how these metal-microbe dynamics influence pathogen virulence, microbial selection pressure, and nutrient-immune interactions in chronic diseases such as endometriosis, chronic kidney disease, Parkinson's disease, and metabolic disorders.

In November 2025, she presented her work on the microbiome signature of endometriosis at the 11th Beneficial Microbes Conference in the Netherlands.

## Standards & Certification

In January 2010, Karen co-founded the Paleo Foundation with her mother, Kimberly Eyer, building it into the leading global certification body for Paleo, Keto, and Grain-Free food products. She has spent over 15 years designing and implementing food certification standards used by manufacturers and brands worldwide.

Her current standards work centers on the Heavy Metal Tested & Certified program, where she is establishing category-specific contaminant limits, ALARA-based principles, and statistical risk matrices for food, supplements, and personal care products. The program covers priority metals including lead, cadmium, arsenic, mercury, nickel, tin, aluminum, and chromium, with consumption-based exposure limits stricter than current U.S. guidance.

## Research Interests

Karen's research interests span multiple interconnected domains:

- Microbial metallomics and metal-microbe dynamics in disease
- Microbiome signatures and microbiome-targeted interventions (MBTIs)
- Heavy metal contamination in food, supplements, and consumer products
- Food safety certification and regulatory strategy
- Trace metal toxicology, bioavailability, and exposure modeling
- The gut-brain axis and neurodegenerative disease (Parkinson's, ferroptosis)
- Endometriosis, chronic kidney disease, and metabolic disorders
- Prebiotic fiber fortification and hydrocolloid science
